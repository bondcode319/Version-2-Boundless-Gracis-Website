Why it matters

The cost is rarely the treatment

A child in Kyegegwa District can usually be treated for very little. What stops a caregiver is everything around the treatment: the fare to the health centre, the day of trading income lost, the drugs the facility has run out of, and the second journey nobody can afford to make for a follow-up.

Giving to the health fund removes those obstacles as a set, which is why we ask for the whole episode of care rather than a single line item.

What the health fund pays for

Coded to the health, nutrition and WASH programme and reported against it. Amounts are indicative and under review against the 2026 baseline survey — all figures TBC.

  • A place at an outreachConsultation, basic drugs and a recorded diagnosis at a camp run jointly with the district health team.
  • Malnutrition screening and feedingMid-upper-arm measurement, supplementary feeding where indicated, and follow-up until the child recovers.
  • Referral transportThe fare that turns a referral letter into an actual admission at a facility that can treat the child.
  • The follow-up visitA case worker returning to the household, because the second visit is the one that decides whether treatment worked.

How your gift is handled

Coded to the fund

Gifts to this appeal are coded to health, nutrition and WASH in our accounts and cannot be quietly moved to cover core costs.

Spent with the district

Outreaches are planned with district health teams and referrals go into the public system, not into a parallel service of ours.

Counted, then reported

Against a 2030 target of 5,000 children screened, treated or referred, and published whether or not we hit it.

Care should not depend on how far you can walk

Give once, or set up a monthly gift that lets a case worker plan a course of treatment rather than react to a crisis.
