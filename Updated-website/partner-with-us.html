Ways to partner

Four kinds of partnership that work

We would rather have one honest multi-year relationship than ten logo placements. Each of these starts with a conversation about what you actually want to achieve.

Corporate partnership

Multi-year programme funding, a named phase of the clinic appeal, payroll giving, matched giving, or employee skills volunteering that matches a real gap.

Institutional funders

Trusts, foundations and bilateral programmes. We will complete your due-diligence questionnaire and report to your format and schedule.

Schools and churches

A school or congregation link-up: a funded class or cohort, a termly update written for your audience, and a speaker for an assembly or service.

In-kind and technical

Scholastic materials, medical consumables, construction materials, or pro-bono technical support in audit, legal, M&E or digital systems.

What you can expect

The things partners actually need from an NGO

  • A named contact who answers, and a reporting schedule we keep to.
  • Restricted funds coded to your fund and reported against it, with no quiet reallocation.
  • A completed due-diligence pack: constitution, organogram, strategic plan, safeguarding policy, finance manual and audited accounts.
  • Safeguarding compliance that will survive your own review, including for any employee volunteering you send.
  • Honest reporting, including the targets we miss and what we changed as a result.

What we ask in return

  • No child used as marketing. Images and stories for your channels come through our consent process, and consent can be withdrawn at any time.
  • Predictability over size. A smaller multi-year commitment is worth more to a case worker than a larger one-off gift.
  • Core costs count. Someone has to pay for the finance officer and the audit. A partnership that funds only visible activity quietly shifts that cost somewhere else.
  • Alignment with our values. We will decline a partnership that conflicts with the best interests of children, and we would rather say that at the start.
