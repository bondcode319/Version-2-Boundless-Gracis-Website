The problem

The household is the real programme

Almost every child on our register lives with a caregiver whose income is seasonal, informal and thin — a widow, a grandmother, an elderly guardian holding several children together, or a teenage mother whose own schooling stopped. When that income fails, school stops first, then meals, then medicine.

An organisation that funds only the child is signing up to fund that child forever. Strengthening the household is how sponsorship becomes finite, and how protection becomes something the community sustains rather than something we supply.

What we do

Income, and the systems that protect it

  • Village Savings and Loan Associations formed and trained, focused on caregivers of supported children.
  • Enterprise and financial literacy training, with start-up or top-up grants on completion.
  • Climate-smart agriculture, kitchen gardens and farmer field schools to hold food security through a bad season.
  • A women’s skilling centre offering tailoring, crafts, catering and other marketable trades.
  • Support for teenage mothers into training, enterprise or a return to education.

How we hold ourselves to it

Income measured at 12 months

Not at the end of the training. Our indicator is the share of households reporting increased income a full year later — target 70% by 2030.

Family-based care, always

We strengthen kinship and foster care and support reunification. Boundless Gracia does not run, and will not build, a residential institution for children.

A working complaints route

Every community structure we train is given the reporting mechanism, the 24-hour rule and the referral path to the Police and the District Probation and Social Welfare Officer.

What a caregiver actually goes through

Four stages, about twelve months

  1. Join a group

    A savings group of caregivers is formed, trained in the rules and given a lockbox, a ledger and an elected committee.

  2. Learn the trade

    Enterprise and financial literacy training, or a place at the women’s skilling centre in tailoring, crafts or catering.

  3. Start up

    A start-up or top-up grant on completion, matched to a simple business plan the group has reviewed.

  4. Twelve-month check

    A year later we ask whether household income actually rose, and we publish the share that says yes — including when it disappoints us.

The goal is a household that no longer needs us

Fund a savings group, a skilling place or a start-up grant, and you are funding the moment support becomes unnecessary rather than permanent.
