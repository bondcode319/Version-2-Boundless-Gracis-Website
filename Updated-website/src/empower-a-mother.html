Why the caregiver

The fastest route to a child staying in school is a mother with an income

Almost every child on our register lives with a caregiver whose income is seasonal, informal and thin: a widow, a grandmother, an elderly guardian holding several children together, or a teenage mother whose own schooling stopped abruptly.

When that income fails, school stops first, then meals, then medicine. Fund the child alone and you have signed up to fund that child forever. Fund the household and sponsorship becomes something a family can eventually do without.

What a place includes

Four things, in this order

The sequence matters. A grant handed over before the training and the savings discipline is a grant spent once.

  1. A savings group place

    Membership of a Village Savings and Loan Association of caregivers, with a lockbox, a ledger, an elected committee and rules the group writes itself.

  2. Enterprise training

    Financial literacy and small-business training, or a place at the women’s skilling centre in tailoring, crafts or catering.

  3. A start-up grant

    Awarded on completion, against a simple business plan the group has reviewed — and topped up where a business is working and ready to grow.

  4. A twelve-month check

    We return a year later and ask whether household income actually rose. The share that says yes is what we publish — our target is 70% by 2030.

What else it buys

A household that is harder to break

  • Food security through a bad season, via kitchen gardens, climate-smart agriculture and farmer field schools.
  • A buffer for the unbudgeted week — the illness, the funeral, the failed harvest — that would otherwise end a child’s schooling.
  • A group of caregivers who know each other, which is also the most reliable child protection network a village has.
  • A route back into learning or enterprise for teenage mothers, whose options narrow fastest and earliest.
