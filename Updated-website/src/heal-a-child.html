Why a pooled fund

Illness does not keep to a budget line

You cannot schedule a child’s malaria. You cannot predict which week a mother will need a referral to the district hospital, or which outreach will find three severely malnourished children instead of none.

So gifts to Heal a Child are pooled and spent as cases present, rather than assigned to one named child in advance. It is the arrangement that gets the medicine to the child who needs it on the day they need it — and it is reported in exactly the same detail.

What it does not do is build a parallel health service. Every outreach is planned with district health teams, and every referral goes into the public system so that a child’s care continues when we are not there.

What the fund pays for

Four costs that stop a caregiver seeking care

In most households it is not the treatment that is unaffordable. It is everything around it.

The consultation

Clinical assessment at an outreach camp or health facility, including malnutrition screening for every child seen.

The medicine

Prescribed treatment dispensed on the day, plus supplementary feeding where a child is screened as malnourished.

The transport

Referral transport to the district facility. Where a household cannot reach a hospital, a referral slip is just a piece of paper.

The follow-up

The visit three weeks later that confirms a child actually recovered — the step most commonly skipped, and the one we count.

What you get back

A coded receipt

Your gift is recorded against the health and WASH fund in our accounts and acknowledged in writing.

The quarterly update

How many children were screened, treated and referred, and how many were seen again at follow-up.

The annual report

The audited position for the fund as a whole, against the results framework and the targets we published.
