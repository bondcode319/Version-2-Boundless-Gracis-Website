The problem

Enrolment is not the finish line

In the communities where we work, children enter school and then leave it. A term’s fees are found; the next term’s are not. A uniform is outgrown. A guardian falls ill and the eldest girl is kept home to nurse and to fetch water. At the transition to secondary school the attrition sharpens, and it sharpens for girls first.

So our measure is not how many children we enrolled. It is how many completed the level they entered, and what they can do afterwards.

What we do

Five activities, one continuous system

  • Sponsorship covering fees, scholastic materials and uniforms, with priority to girls and children with disabilities.
  • Early childhood development and remedial learning support, to cut repetition and dropout at the roots.
  • Vocational and technical skills training with start-up kits for out-of-school youth and teenage mothers.
  • Mentorship, life-skills and career guidance delivered in partnership with schools.
  • Work with school management committees to improve the learning environment and reduce corporal punishment and school-related gender-based violence.

How we hold ourselves to it

Completion, not enrolment

Our headline indicator is the percentage of supported children who complete the level they enrolled in. Target: 90% by 2030, measured against the 2026 baseline.

A case worker, not a cheque

Every sponsored child has a named case worker who visits the household, tracks attendance and results, and intervenes before a child leaves rather than after.

Safe schools, not just funded ones

We will not keep paying fees into a school that will not address corporal punishment or school-related gender-based violence. That is a condition, not a conversation.
