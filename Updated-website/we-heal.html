The problem

Distance is the diagnosis

A treatable fever becomes an emergency when the health centre is a half-day walk and the household has no transport money. Malnutrition goes unnoticed until a child is visibly wasting. An expectant mother delivers at home because the clinic is unreachable after dark.

Our health work exists to shorten that distance — physically, through outreaches and water points close to home, and financially, by removing the cost that stops a caregiver seeking care at all.

What we do

Five activities, one continuous system

  • Community health outreaches and medical camps run jointly with district health teams.
  • Malnutrition screening with supplementary feeding or referral, and follow-up until a child recovers.
  • Immunisation outreach, antenatal care and skilled-delivery mobilisation and referral.
  • Safe water points constructed or rehabilitated, with household sanitation and hygiene promotion.
  • Menstrual health education and supplies, so that a period never costs a girl a week of school.

How we hold ourselves to it

Consent before publicity

No identifiable image or personal detail of a child is published without documented, informed guardian consent. Every staff member, volunteer and visiting partner signs our safeguarding policy.

With the district, not beside it

Outreaches are planned with district health teams and referrals go into the public system. We are not building a parallel service that collapses when funding moves on.

Counted, then reported

Every child screened, treated or referred is recorded against our results framework, reviewed quarterly by the Board and published in the annual report and audited accounts.
