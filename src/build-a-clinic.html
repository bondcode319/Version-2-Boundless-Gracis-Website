The project

A building, so the care stops being temporary

Today our health work at Mpara runs from canopies and borrowed rooms. A mobile camp can screen a hundred children in a day, but it cannot receive a mother in labour at two in the morning, cannot hold a cold chain, and cannot keep a patient file that a clinician will read again in six months.

The Board has approved a single-storey unit of six rooms: a consultation room, a treatment and dressing room, a maternal and antenatal room with a delivery space, a small laboratory, a pharmacy store with a solar-powered cold chain, and a covered waiting area with a rainwater tank and a latrine block.

The land has been secured. Every figure below comes from the quantity surveyor’s bill of quantities and is reviewed by the Finance, Audit and Administration Committee of the Board. Costs are placeholders pending final tender — TBC.
