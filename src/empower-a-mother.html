Why it matters

Sponsorship should be finite

Almost every child on our register lives with a caregiver whose income is seasonal, informal and thin — a widow, a grandmother, an elderly guardian holding several children together. When that income fails, school stops first, then meals, then medicine.

An organisation that funds only the child is signing up to fund that child forever. This appeal funds the other half: the household’s own ability to pay, so our support can eventually stop.

What the livelihoods fund pays for

Coded to the livelihoods and protection programme and reported against it. Amounts are indicative and under review against the 2026 baseline survey — all figures TBC.

  • A place in a savings groupFormation, training and the record-keeping that makes a Village Savings and Loan Association survive its first year.
  • Enterprise and financial literacy trainingPricing, stock, margin and record-keeping — taught to caregivers of children we already support.
  • A start-up or top-up grantReleased on completion of training, against a plan the caregiver has written and a group has witnessed.
  • A place at the skilling centreTailoring, crafts, catering and other marketable trades for caregivers with no reliable income at all.

How your gift is handled

Measured at 12 months

Not at the end of the training. The indicator is the share of households reporting increased income a full year later — target 70% by 2030.

Family-based care, always

We strengthen kinship and foster care and support reunification. BGCF does not run, and will not build, a residential institution for children.

A working complaints route

Every group we train is given the reporting mechanism, the 24-hour rule and the referral path to Police and the District Probation and Social Welfare Officer.

The goal is a household that no longer needs us

Fund a savings group, a skilling place or a start-up grant, and you are funding the moment sponsorship becomes unnecessary rather than permanent.
